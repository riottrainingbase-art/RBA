# RTB SELECT — ZERO-INVENTORY OPERATING PLAYBOOK

Updated: 2026-10-02
Status: PILOT / PREVIEW
Owner: Riot Training Base

## 1. Purpose

RTB SELECT is not a conventional sporting-goods store. It is a demand-led retail layer inside Riot Training Base.

Core rule:

> Ask first. Sell second. Buy third. Stock only what proves demand.

RTB is the physical entrance and training context.
RBA is a basketball category and a distribution/event channel.
Original/OEM products are introduced only after repeatable demand is visible.

## 2. Customer promise

TRAIN / RECOVER / WEAR

- TRAIN: training apparel, shorts, socks, small training goods
- RECOVER: self-care and recovery products RTB can explain and demonstrate
- WEAR: performance-lifestyle products usable beyond training
- COURT: basketball-specific products, mainly through RBA

Do not become a general sporting-goods shop.
Do not compete on assortment breadth.
Compete on selection, context, demonstration and trust.

## 3. Phase 0 — zero-cash validation

No speculative inventory.

Allowed models, in priority order:
1. Pre-order / order-after-payment
2. Consignment
3. Sample display + order
4. One-unit / small-lot wholesale
5. Pop-up
6. Team order
7. POD test for RTB originals

Avoid:
- minimum opening orders that create slow-moving size/color stock
- footwear inventory
- broad apparel size runs
- large OEM minimums
- paid ads before organic conversion is known

## 4. Pilot funnel

Traffic sources:
- RTB customers and visitors
- RTB Google Business Profile / organic local search
- RTB LINE and direct contacts
- RBA audience where the product is relevant
- RBA events, camps and team contacts
- brand partner referrals

Flow:
1. /rtb-select landing page
2. Early-access / request form
3. Aggregate demand by category / brand / price / size
4. Negotiate only the most requested products
5. Offer a 5–10 day preorder window
6. Collect payment before purchase where contract terms permit
7. Place order
8. Deliver at RTB or ship
9. Ask for product feedback
10. Decide restock / stop / OEM

## 6. Decision gates

### Gate A — Launch a test offer
Proceed when one category has either:
- 10+ qualified requests, or
- 5+ respondents naming the same/similar product, or
- a team/event request that creates 10+ units of likely demand.

### Gate B — Hold physical stock
Only consider stock when:
- the same SKU/near-equivalent sells in at least two separate order windows, and
- expected 30-day sell-through is 60%+, and
- gross margin after inbound freight/payment fees is acceptable.

Default opening stock: 0–2 display/sample units, not a full size run.

### Gate C — Create RTB OEM
Only proceed when:
- category demand is proven across at least two drops, and
- 30+ units can be pre-sold or strongly committed, and
- target landed gross margin is 60%+,
- design is not dependent on a short-lived trend.

First OEM candidates:
1. Socks
2. Tee
3. Towel / small accessory
4. Shorts

Do not begin with hoodies, jackets or complex size-heavy products.

## 7. Margin rules

For every offer calculate:

Landed cost =
wholesale/OEM cost
+ inbound freight
+ packaging
+ payment fee
+ expected handling/returns allowance

Gross profit = selling price - landed cost
Gross margin = gross profit / selling price

Pilot targets:
- third-party select: seek 40%+ gross margin where feasible
- OEM: seek 60%+ landed gross margin
- team/bulk: accept lower percentage only if gross-profit yen and inventory risk are attractive

Never approve a product only because the nominal wholesale discount looks good.

## 11. Initial numeric targets

First proof target:
- 30 qualified survey/early-access responses
- 10 paid orders
- ¥50,000+ sales
- positive gross profit
- no more than ¥10,000 of unsold speculative inventory

Next proof:
- 25 paid orders/month
- AOV ¥5,000+
- monthly sales ¥125,000+
- gross margin 40%+
- repeat/order-again signal from 20%+ of buyers

Scale only after proof.

## 12. Content plan

Do not post generic product ads.

Content types:
- Why RTB selected it
- What problem/use case it fits
- Who does not need it
- How it feels/works in actual training
- Size/fit notes
- Test-user feedback
- preorder deadline
- sold-out / next request notice

Tone:
plain, practical, no hype, no fake scarcity.

## 13. Physical entrance

Keep the entrance minimal.

Initial setup:
- one sample rail OR one compact shelf
- 3–5 sample products maximum
- one RTB SELECT card/sign
- QR to /rtb-select
- no cartons or size stock visible

The physical space is a showroom and consultation point, not a warehouse.

## 14. Stop rules

Stop or do not reorder when:
- fewer than 3 paid orders after a full qualified launch window
- gross margin is too low after freight/fees
- product needs too many size/color SKUs
- supplier requires excessive opening stock
- supplier does not allow the intended sales channel
- customer support/returns create disproportionate work
- product does not strengthen TRAIN / RECOVER / WEAR

## 15. Data integrity

Every product decision must be supported by:
- requests
- paid orders
- actual margin
- sell-through
- customer feedback

Views, likes and compliments are not demand.
