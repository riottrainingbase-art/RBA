# RBA Content Operating System


## Purpose


Turn real work in Japanese gyms into credible, useful evidence of how RBA thinks and operates. Content exists to document development, help coaches think, and open relevant conversations across Asia. It must never become hype, child ranking, or a substitute for safeguarding.


## One activity, seven outputs


For every clinic, camp, coach meeting or exchange:


1. Japan Field Note — context, observation, adaptation, next question.
2. 60-second vertical film — one problem and one learning point.
3. Photo sequence — court, equipment, hands, whiteboard and non-identifiable action unless consent is confirmed.
4. RBA Radio reflection — 5–10 minutes recorded after the activity.
5. Coach insight card — three precise takeaways without “secret drill” language.
6. Email dispatch — one useful observation and a link to the full note.
7. Partner case study — only when names, logos, quotes and media use are separately approved.


## Field capture card


Complete this within 30 minutes after delivery:


- Location / date / age group / format
- Learning problem selected before practice
- What information players needed to perceive
- What players first did
- What the coach changed in the environment
- What changed after the adaptation
- What remained unresolved
- One sentence for parents
- One question for another Asian coach
- Media permission status: confirmed / limited / not confirmed


## Editorial structure


Every field note follows:


1. Context — enough to understand the environment, without exposing children.
2. Observation — behaviour seen, not a judgement about a child.
3. Adaptation — what changed in the practice design.
4. Evidence — what was actually observed after the change.
5. Next question — what RBA will test or discuss next.


## Language rules


Use:


- player-centred
- age-appropriate
- practice design
- perception, decision and execution
- observe, compare, adapt
- physical preparation grounded in science
- long-term development


Avoid:


- secret drill / ultimate method
- guaranteed improvement
- Japan is superior
- future star / elite child labels
- unsupported federation or national-team authority
- medical, injury or performance claims without appropriate evidence and scope
- fake urgency, countdowns and exaggerated scarcity


## Safeguarding and permission gate


Do not publish until all relevant items are confirmed:


- Child face, name, voice and identifying information
- Parent or guardian media consent
- Venue permission where required
- Partner name and logo use
- Guest recording and quotation approval
- Health, injury and return-to-play information removed or appropriately authorised
- No private schedules, contact data or travel details exposed


When permission is absent, use empty-court imagery, equipment, diagrams, anonymous observations, coach-only audio and non-identifying details.


## Brand asset rule


The official monochrome Riot Basketball Academy badge is the primary identifier. Keep its proportions intact, do not redraw the basketball or lettering, and do not place text or patterns over it. The website uses the original supplied badge in the global header and footer.


## Radio production gate


An episode can move from development to published only after:


- Guest and recording permission
- Name, role and biographical claims verified
- Audio edit reviewed
- Music and media rights confirmed
- Child-identifying audio removed
- Episode title and description proofread
- Links tested on mobile
- Transcript checked for encoding issues
- Final guest approval where agreed


## Monthly publishing rhythm


- Week 1: Japan Field Note + 60-second film
- Week 2: Coach insight + email dispatch
- Week 3: RBA Radio episode or solo field reflection
- Week 4: Asia question — invite one relevant coach to compare context


The rhythm is a quality target, not a reason to publish weak or unapproved material.


## Success measures


Prioritise signals of professional trust:


- Relevant academy or coach replies
- Qualified collaboration conversations
- Field-note completion rate
- Email replies and saves
- Audio completion where available
- Invitations to exchange information
- Paid reviews, practice-design work, audits or pilot discussions


Follower count is supporting evidence, not the main result.
